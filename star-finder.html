Scientific principle

Reading the live charts

A star's place in the sky is fixed by right ascension and declination, two celestial coordinates expressed in time and arc. The device measures orientation with its magnetometer and gyroscope, then converts the observer's latitude, longitude and sidereal time into altitude and azimuth for each target. The live projection therefore changes as the sky rotates.

Practical applications

The finder supports night-sky education, telescope alignment, deep-sky photography planning and outdoor orientation. Educators use it to demonstrate celestial coordinate mathematics, while field users rely on it for locating planets and deep-sky objects.

Accuracy and disclaimer

This tool is for educational purposes and should not replace professional calibration equipment. Live values depend on device sensors and local magnetic or environmental conditions.
