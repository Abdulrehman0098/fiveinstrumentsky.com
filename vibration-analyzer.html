The science behind the measurement

Vibration analysis starts with acceleration. An accelerometer measures proper acceleration including gravity, producing three axes of metres per second squared. Severity metrics such as RMS, peak and crest factor describe the amplitude distribution, while a fast Fourier transform separates the signal into frequency components.

Frequency information matters because the same acceleration level can correspond to very different physical behaviour depending on where it occurs in the spectrum. Resonance, harmonics and sub-structuring all require spectral context.

How to use the Vibration Analyzer

Start the measurement, apply the sensitivity setting, and review the three-axis waveform plus the FFT spectrum. Use the events view to flag thresholds, and compare the axis with the largest magnitude before drawing conclusions.

Interpreting live data

The waveform chart plots acceleration across the axes over time, while the FFT chart maps magnitude against frequency. Peaks and bands reveal the source, and the events view can surface thresholds. Use the axis and frequency readout together for diagnosis.

Practical applications

Engineers use vibration analysis for machinery health, structural monitoring, vehicle ride quality, sports equipment diagnostics and occupational exposure assessment.

Accuracy and disclaimer

This tool is for educational purposes and should not replace professional calibration equipment. Sensitivity settings and mounting influence readings.
